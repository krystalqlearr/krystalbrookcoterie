# FORM 2 · DISCOVERY

## Part One — The Business

**1. Describe your business as you'd describe it to your dream client at a dinner party.**
I design and hand-build custom-coded websites for founder-led beauty, med-spa, wellness, bridal, and luxury lifestyle brands. Not templates, not Squarespace — real code, on the same stack the biggest tech companies run. I work through an AI-augmented studio, which means a brand gets senior-level design and engineering judgment on every decision, at a speed and price a traditional agency can't touch. The result is a site the brand actually owns, built to compound in value instead of aging into a liability.

**2. What do you sell, at what price points, and what's the most profitable thing you offer?**
Four fully custom-coded tiers: Launch $4,500 · Signature $9,800 · Atelier $22,000+ · Atelier Custom $32,000+. Monthly Care Plans: Essential $175 · Growth $450 · Partner $950. A Founding Client program offers Signature-tier scope at $2,800 in exchange for case-study rights. Most profitable per hour: Atelier and Atelier Custom, because the AI-augmented process compresses build time while scope and price scale up. Highest lifetime value: Care Plan clients — recurring revenue that compounds.

**3. Where does revenue actually come from today?**
Early stage. The near-term engine is a 90-day Loom-based outreach campaign to ~3 Sacramento-area luxury service businesses, plus platform presence (Contra, Webflow Experts, Framer Partner Program). Long-term: referrals and the compounding proof of published case studies.

**4. Twelve months after launch, what makes this website an undeniable success?**
[confirm targets] Draft: 3–4 Founding Clients converted from the first outreach quarter; the site consistently converting qualified inquiries (target proposal-to-close above 40%); 2–3 published case studies live; a small stack of Care Plan clients producing recurring revenue; inbound inquiries that already understand the custom-code positioning before the first call.

**5. What does this website need to do that a template never could?**
Be the proof itself. The site has to *be* the argument for custom code — fast, distinctive, editorially precise in ways a Showit or Squarespace site structurally can't be. It's the studio's flagship demonstration; a template site would quietly undercut the entire pitch.

## Part Two — The People I Serve

**6. Best client ever (the profile to attract):**
A founder-led luxury brand doing roughly $150K–$5M, in beauty, med-spa, wellness, bridal, or luxury lifestyle — Sacramento-area or national — who has outgrown a template and *knows* it. They care how things feel, have a real aesthetic point of view, are ready to invest in something owned, and value senior judgment over a cheap deliverable.

**7. The client to attract less of:**
Price shoppers. Anyone who wants to "just tweak my template," needs it "by Friday," or decides by committee. The site should quietly repel these as intentionally as it attracts the right ones.

**8. What prospects always ask / their main hesitation:**
"Why custom instead of Squarespace/Showit?" and "Is AI-augmented a shortcut — am I getting less of you, not more?" The hesitation is usually price justification and trust. The site answers both: rented vs. owned, and AI-as-asset (senior judgment on everything, nothing handed to juniors). AI Is the TOOL not the developer. Just as a carpenter now uses a drill more than a screwdriver, KBC uses AI to complete projects more timely and on budget.

**9. What happy clients say (voice to echo):**

# FORM 3 · BRAND & AESTHETIC

**1. Brand assets on hand:**
Full 7-token color system, PP Editorial New + PP Pangram Sans (Pangram Pangram v7.72, licensed), three wordmark lockups (stacked, horizontal, monogram), the terracotta-with-teal-ring custom cursor, and the complete design direction. [confirm: Pangram Pangram client-deployment license terms in writing before using the fonts on client sites — studio-site use is covered.]

**2. Brand style guide:** Yes — locked and documented (this brief + the design direction file are it).

**3. Three words the brand IS:** Editorial. Owned. Precise.
**Three words it is NOT:** Templated. Loud. Trend-chasing.

**4. If the brand were a physical space:**
A dimly, warmly lit atelier at dusk. Rich black walls, a single band of deep petrol like lacquer near the floor. Cream light pooling on a long left-aligned worktable. Terracotta as the one warm object in the room — a ceramic, a spine, a thread — and a fine teal line inlaid into the surfaces, structural, never decorative. Quiet. Considered. Nothing extra.

**5. Websites/references loved — and why:**
The Row (restraint, luxury through space). BORN Group (editorial confidence, asymmetric nerve). The through-line: generous whitespace, left-aligned editorial composition, type doing the heavy lifting, imagery placed with intent rather than filling grids.

**6. References that feel wrong:**
Centered template stacking, pastel "feminine web design" defaults, page-builder blocks, the cream-background-plus-washed-terracotta look that now reads as generic AI design. KBC is dark-led and its terracotta is a deeper oxide (#BF6940), deliberately not that default.

**7. How someone should feel in the first five seconds:**
Impressed and taken care of — the sense of being in genuinely expert hands, of restraint that signals confidence.

**8. Brand on the axes:**
Minimal◄──── (toward minimal, with editorial expression) · Classic ───►Modern (modern) · Warm/Cool held in tension (warm accents on a cool dark canvas) · Understated ────►Bold (bold typographically, restrained everywhere else) · Soft ────►Structured (structured) · Playful ────►Serious (serious, with warmth).

**9. Colors — love / never:**
Love: the 7 locked tokens. Rules that are non-negotiable — petrol is footer-only, teal is structural-only (rules, dividers, markers, cursor ring), terracotta is the single warm accent, greige carries eyebrow/muted text (and specifically eyebrows on the petrol footer for AA contrast). Never: altering global token values to solve a contrast problem — choose a compliant token instead.

**10. Visual signatures that must carry through:**
Left-alignment as default. Asymmetric, offset imagery for editorial contrast. Teal structural rules. The italic accent word inside display headings (PP Editorial New italic). The terracotta cursor with the teal hover ring. Dark-led canvas throughout.

**11. Anything to keep from a current site:** N/A — new build. The one thing to carry from the Glowtoure build is the proof of the standard: fast, custom-coded, editorially restrained.

**12. Biggest fear about this project:**
That the studio site reads as "another pretty template" and the custom-code differentiator gets lost. Design around it: make the site itself so precise and fast that the argument is self-evident, and state the rented-vs-owned line plainly.
